# Meta Ad Delivery System — Andromeda, Lattice, GEM

**Source:** [@mr.paidsocial TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@mr.paidsocial/video/7600101862116805918)
**Added:** 2026-09-10
**Tags:** meta, delivery, roas, creative, targeting

## The 4 Layers (stacked, each adds measurable lift)

1. **Andromeda — Candidate Filtering (+8% ROAS lift)**
   Filters the full ad pool down to relevant candidates per user. Better creative signals = more people see your ad in the first place. This is the retrieval layer.

2. **Lattice — Cross-Advertiser Learning (+6% ROAS lift)**
   Shares conversion signals across advertisers. If another brand's ad converted a user, Meta uses that for your targeting too. Why broad targeting beats interest stacking — you borrow from the whole ecosystem's signal.

3. **Sequential Learning — Auto-Sequence (+3% ROAS lift)**
   Automatically sequences creatives per user based on funnel stage. Don't kill high-spend ads mid-run — you reset this learning. A "tired" ad that still has budget is still teaching the system.

4. **GEM — LLM Per-User Prediction (+5% ROAS lift)**
   A large language model predicts conversion probability per user per ad in real time. This is why **creative IS targeting** — GEM reads your ad content to match it to users, not just your audience settings.

## Key Rules
- Never kill a high-spend ad mid-run — let it exhaust naturally or duplicate + iterate
- Creative IS targeting (via GEM). Write for the person you want, the LLM does the matching
- Campaign-level budget > ad set budget — gives Meta liquidity to route across all 4 systems
- Broad targeting > interest stacking because Lattice works better with a wider candidate pool

## Orbit Application
Orbit's recommendations layer should diagnose WHICH component is underperforming vs benchmarks, not just flag "ROAS is down." Output should be specific: "Your CTR is 0.4% vs 1.2% category benchmark — the issue is the hook, not the landing page."
